Sketching

For the grown-up · read this first, then hand over the prompt pages

Sketching is the hobby most people quit in week one because of one sentence: "I can't draw." The prompt pages in this kit are built to dodge that sentence. Nothing on them requires drawing well — they only require drawing at all, badly if needed, with a pencil that's already in a drawer.

How to set it up to succeed

  • A five-minute rule beats a masterpiece rule. The prompts are small and quick on purpose. One prompt per sitting.
  • You draw too. Two bad drawings together is a memory; one supervised good drawing is a chore.
  • Praise honesty over quality: "the dog really does have that many legs in your drawing" lands better than generic "nice!"
  • Never say "don't erase." Erasing is practice too.

What counts as done: the prompt is attempted, the date is written, and the sketcher has enjoyed the process for five minutes. That's the whole criteria.

The no-gear rule

Any pencil and any paper do. If you want to level up later, a cheap spiral sketchbook and a soft 2B pencil are the only upgrades worth making — and the printed pages here work fine in a binder or taped to the fridge, which is honestly where the best drawings earn their place.

Print page 2 (prompt grid) once, then as copies of page 3 as needed — one page holds two finished prompts.

Thirty Tiny Sketch Prompts

Pick any box. Five minutes per sketch. Cross boxes off in any order — no wrong order exists.

1. Your shoe, front view 2. A mug in one line, no lifting pencil 3. The view out the nearest window
4. A hand — your other one 5. A crumpled piece of paper 6. A plant, including the pot
7. Something purple you can see 8. Your dinner, before eating it 9. A doorknob up close
10. A leaf with its shadow 11. A pet, sleeping if possible 12. The chair you're sitting in
13. A cloud, drawn in 30 seconds 14. Keys on a keyring 15. A building's corner from below
16. Three random objects in one line-up 17. A self-portrait with eyes closed 18. Something broken about, mended
19. A jar's contents 20. An insect you saw today 21. A thing on the highest shelf
22. A bicycle or scooter in one breath 23. Texture: bark, brick, blanket 24. A full traffic scene at a road
25. Lamps and their shadows 26. A book's spine, stack of three 27. The messiest corner in the house
28. A tiny doodle of your morning 29. A stranger's silhouette (no faces) 30. The drawing on your last 3 free minutes

Streak rule: five boxes crossed in one week earns a page printed again. Thirty crossed boxes means the sketcher has officially started a hobby.
